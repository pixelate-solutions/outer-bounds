Bill of materials: Outer Bounds Pro & Outer Bounds Indoor

This is the complete, final parts list for both products. There are no phases and no reduced versions. Both units share the same sensing core: three IMX900 global-shutter cameras, 24 strobed 940 nm LEDs, two certified 24 GHz radar heads and a Qualcomm QCS6490 compute module with Wi-Fi 6E. The Pro adds the swappable 81 Wh battery, a sunlight-readable display, an inclinometer and an IP55 blower. The Indoor adds Gigabit Ethernet and a 24 V supply. Every line has a manufacturer part number, a vendor and a price at four build volumes. Each line is either orderable today, quote-only, or a custom part that needs our design files.

"BOM" = all parts, boards, enclosure, box contents and assembly/test labour per unit. "Landed" adds 3% scrap, 10% US duty (Taiwan final assembly) and $12 freight. One-time tooling, NRE and certification () are listed separately. Spread over a 10,000-unit product life, they add per unit.

Product-manager decisions

Where the research turned up a price, supply or engineering problem, this is what I chose and why.

Compute: Qualcomm QCS6490 (Thundercomm C6490P), not RK3588

The RK3588 module is about $185 at 1k but needs a separate Wi-Fi module (~$11). The QCS6490 module is $190 with Wi-Fi 6E and BT 5.2 already on board, carries FCC/CE, has a published lifecycle to 2036 (Rockchip gives no longevity commitment), offers 5 four-lane camera ports and draws phone-class power. Lower power is what makes the 6-hour runtime and the sealed 40 °C box work. Gap: no board has a shipping IMX900 triggered-mono driver except Jetson. A paid driver port is budgeted ($40k NRE). Backup: Jetson Orin Nano 8GB ($399, drivers exist). Alt: Forlinx FET3588J-C.

Cameras are a third of the BOM, so there's a cost-down path

Three Vision Components IMX900 mono modules: about $230 each in prototype quantities, €109.99 (≈$128) catalog at 1k. A one-time custom-module NRE with VC/e-con (~$30k) brings them to ~$95 at 1k. At 10k, our own sensor board with active lens alignment reaches ~$65. The club camera stays an IMX900 rather than a cheaper OV9281: that saves only ~$53 and costs resolution plus a second driver and calibration model. Open question: IMX900 "quad shutter" can't be confirmed without Sony's NDA datasheet, so the design runs on plain triggered single exposures.

Swaps for price and stock

  • TI SN74LVC1G123 one-shot (0 stock, $0.83) → Nexperia 74LVC1G123DC ($0.22 @1k)
  • TI LM74700 ideal diode (0 stock, $1.23) → Diodes AP74700 ($0.45)
  • TI TPS25750 PD controller (not recommended for new designs) → TPS25751
  • OSRAM SFH 4727AS LED (not for new designs) → Vishay VSMA1094250, about 25% cheaper, with OSRAM SFH 4728AS on a dual footprint
  • OmniPreSense OPS243 radar ($224, no volume price) → 2× InnoSenT SMR-333 ($13.80 @1k, FCC module grant)
  • Nanuk hard case in every box ($100–150) → molded EVA case ($18 @1k). Nanuk is sold as a premium accessory
  • STM32G474 QFN (52-week lead) → LQFP package (in stock)

Battery: custom 81 Wh smart pack

Standard formats fail: NP-F has no UN38.3 or fuel gauge, V-mount is too big at $130–160, tool packs carry legal risk, LP-E6 is 16 Wh. The custom pack is 4× LG M58T 21700 cells with a TI BQ40Z50 fuel gauge, user-swappable for EU 2027 rules and under 100 Wh for flights: $57 @1k, $35 @10k. First builds use the certified Inspired Energy ND4054HD50 ($167). Runtime: 81 Wh gives ~5.5–6 h only if the system averages ≤12 W. That budget is the firmware power team's target (QCS6490 helps). At 15 W it is ~4.7 h.
